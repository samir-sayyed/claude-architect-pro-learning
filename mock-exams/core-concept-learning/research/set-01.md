## Verified corrections and limits

1. **Batch deadlines:** video 1 at 62:42 suggests all batches complete within 24 hours and treats next-morning delivery as satisfied. The opened batch documentation explicitly permits expiry without completion after 24 hours. A six-hour deadline needs measured behavior and fallback. Q11 tests that correction.
2. **Cache thresholds:** video 3 at 27:15 and other passages describe 512 tokens as a general minimum. The opened caching table has model-dependent minima of 512, 1,024, 2,048 and 4,096. Q18 stipulates that the selected model is eligible and tests prefix identity, avoiding a universal numeric minimum. Cache prices also vary; Q40 explicitly supplies fictional prices rather than treating one historical read multiplier as universal.
3. **Context capacity:** blanket claims that every current or available model has a million-token window should be qualified. The opened context-window page says capacity depends on the model and still lists 200k examples. No item infers model capability from a universal window size or treats fit as proof of good recall.
4. **Bundled experiments:** video 1 at 56:36 overstates the need to change exactly one component before any causal conclusion. A randomized package comparison can estimate the package effect; it cannot isolate the prompt contribution. Q36 makes that distinction explicit. This is experimental-design reasoning, not a verbatim vendor rule.
5. **Repeated-run uncertainty:** video 1 at 58:00 offers a one-over-square-root rule using cases times repeats. That is not a universal exact margin for paired differences with case-level dependence. Q37 requires case-aware paired uncertainty and does not invent a universal significance threshold. Repeated runs are not automatically independent new cases.
6. **Human-judge thresholds:** the videos suggest approximately 90% agreement as a broad target. This form uses task-specific consequence, independent labeling and ambiguity handling instead of certifying reliability from a universal percentage. Q34 asks for relevant error directions and demonstrated scope.
7. **Structured output and hooks:** syntax compliance does not establish business correctness, and the opened structured-output docs describe refusal and truncation exceptions. The hooks reference says PostToolUse follows tool execution and does not fire for every possible filesystem writer under an Edit|Write matcher. Q61 retains an enforced acceptance check rather than promising that hook configuration alone guarantees formatted accepted code.
8. **Compliance and fairness:** Q47 stipulates the privacy owner’s valid erasure scope and absence of exceptions. Q48 stipulates the procurement boundary and unapproved telemetry destination rather than asserting blanket current FedRAMP routes. Q49 stipulates the foundation’s policy. No universal legal conclusion or model-specific compliance promise is inferred from the videos.
9. **Exam readiness:** the videos’ 46-correct target and 55-correct booking suggestion are presenter study heuristics. They are not an official raw-to-scaled conversion or validated readiness predictor. Form difficulty is intentionally applied and uncalibrated; no empirical pass prediction is claimed.
