LESSON 01 / OBJECTIVES 6.1 / ~16 MIN

Discover the work before specifying the AI

Domain 6 is the discipline of making an AI system useful, understandable and operable by people who did not build it. The central skill is turning an ambiguous request into decisions that someone can implement, measure and own. The official guide allocates 14% to this domain; its five objectives span discovery, decisions, feedback, documentation and the service lifecycle.

Project convention. The nutrition assistant, agricultural labour marketplace and enterprise support copilot below are hypothetical extensions inspired by familiar product types. Their workflows, numbers and constraints are teaching fixtures, not claims about NutriMind, Krishi Mazdoor or any existing repository. All numerical targets are examples to negotiate and validate.

The feature request hides a job and a failure

A stakeholder says, “Add a nutrition chatbot.” That tells you an interface, not the problem. Ask them to walk through their last difficult meal-planning task: what started it, which information was missing, who checked the result, and what happened next? Observe a complete attempt. A user may already find recipes quickly but struggle to reconcile an allergy, a budget and the ingredients at home. A chatbot that produces recipes faster leaves the hard work untouched.

Discovery should include the existing service, support staff, offline steps and users who need assistance. GOV.UK's discovery guidance explicitly includes users with disabilities and low digital skills. This is useful design evidence beyond government contexts. D6-S01

A practical discovery conversation

  • Outcome: What must the person accomplish, and who receives the benefit?
  • Frequency and variation: How often does the task occur? What changes by language, season, disability, connectivity or account role?
  • Consequences: Which mistakes are inconvenient, costly, irreversible or dangerous?
  • Evidence: Which records or observations establish the current failure rate and effort?
  • Authority: Who may act, who can approve an exception, and who owns an unresolved case?
  • Alternatives: Could a clearer form, search filter or deterministic calculation solve the bottleneck?

Worked case: a voice assistant for farm hiring

A sponsor wants voice-based matching because farmers dislike typing. Interviewing only farmers would miss the labourer's problem: unclear wages, travel distance and whether work is genuinely confirmed. Observe both sides. A useful initial requirement might be “read back wage, location and date in the chosen language before either party commits.” The evidence could reveal that a structured confirmation screen plus optional speech is enough. Voice generation alone does not establish agreement.

Record discoveries as observations, hypotheses and unresolved questions separately. “Three observed users misread the date” is evidence. “All rural users prefer voice” is an unsupported generalization. A discovery brief should make that distinction visible. The first deliverable is a shared account of the work, including a baseline and the uncertainty that the next experiment must reduce.

LESSON 02 / OBJECTIVES 6.1, 6.2, 6.3 / ~15 MIN

Turn competing expectations into an acceptance contract

Requirements are not all equivalent. A constraint defines the feasible designs: tenant isolation, an approved deployment region, a staffed review process or an absolute prohibition on autonomous payment. An acceptance criterion turns a need into an observable pass condition. An optimization goal ranks feasible choices, such as lower cost or faster responses. A hypothesis is a belief awaiting evidence. Mixing these categories makes a cheap but prohibited design appear attractive.

Example discovery statements converted into reviewable requirements

Map people who carry different consequences

The buyer wants lower cost; the support agent wants fewer corrections; the customer wants resolution; security wants limited authority; operations wants a recoverable service. Affected people may have no purchasing power. In a labour marketplace, a worker whose booking is cancelled is a stakeholder even if only the farm pays the platform. Include accessibility and language needs in the primary journey rather than as optional polish.

NIST AI RMF calls for documented accountability, stakeholder context and roles in human-AI oversight. It is voluntary guidance, not a certification that makes a design compliant. Its lifecycle functions are iterative, not a mandatory one-way checklist. D6-S03

Give decisions an owner and a boundary

Use a small responsibility table. The product owner accepts usefulness targets and scope; the domain expert owns the content rubric; engineering owns implementation; operations accepts operating readiness; the designated risk owner decides residual-risk exceptions within their authority. RACI can help: responsible does the work, accountable owns the decision, consulted contributes expertise, informed receives the outcome. Prefer one accountable decision owner for each explicit decision; “everyone approved the slide deck” is not an exception policy.

An acceptance contract can be concise: “For an authenticated support agent, draft a policy-grounded reply to an eligible ticket; never send it; show source versions; route missing-policy cases to the named queue; capture review time.” This identifies actor, task, boundary, evidence and fallback. Add owners and measurable thresholds in the SLO document. A promise becomes implementable when its failure path is also specified.

For exam scenarios, locate the unresolved decision. More interviews help when requirements are unclear; they do not replace an already-required launch authorization. A better model does not remove a conflicting policy. A detailed design is premature while nobody has accepted responsibility for the consequence it creates.

LESSON 03 / OBJECTIVES 6.2 / ~18 MIN

Compare architectures with feasibility, economics and uncertainty

Architecture is choosing which work the system should do, how much authority it needs and which failures the organization can absorb. Anthropic distinguishes fixed workflows from agents that dynamically direct tools and recommends adding complexity when simpler approaches fail to meet the task. That is an architecture starting point, not a rule that agents are always inappropriate. D6-S04

Filter constraints before ranking options

Worked case: support drafting architecture

Assume an independently reviewed pilot measures these configurations on the same task mix. Acceptance requires at least 94% rubric correctness and p95 end-to-end latency at most 4 seconds; approved-region deployment is mandatory. Monthly cost includes review effort and operations. Treat the point estimates as provisional until sample size and uncertainty are assessed.

Hypothetical measured options; all figures are instructional

B fails latency, C fails correctness and D fails the region constraint. E dominates A on these measured dimensions: equal correctness, faster and cheaper. Choose E provisionally if the evidence is trustworthy and there are no omitted material constraints. A weighted score that rewards D's price cannot compensate for its prohibited region. If E relies on an unstaffed operational dependency, add that constraint and reconsider.

Pareto trade-offs and sensitivity

A choice is Pareto-dominated when another feasible choice is at least as good on all relevant dimensions and better on one. If one design is more accurate but slower, neither necessarily dominates: the accountable owner must accept the trade-off. The frontier depends on what you measured. Omitted maintainability, migration cost or review capacity can reverse a conclusion.

Now test the economics. Suppose 8,000 cases occur monthly; adoption is 60%; gross handling-time savings are 4 minutes per adopted case, but corrections add 1 minute. Net capacity released is 8,000 × 0.60 × (4 − 1) / 60 = 240 staff-hours. At an internal planning rate of $25/hour, that is $6,000 of capacity value. Subtract $1,500 operating cost to obtain $4,500 of modeled net value. This is not $4,500 cash saved unless staffing, overtime or spending actually changes.

If adoption is 30% instead, capacity is 120 hours and the same planning model yields $1,500 net value. If correction time rises to 3 minutes at 30% adoption, capacity is only 40 hours, worth $1,000 against $1,500 cost: modeled net value becomes −$500. This sensitivity is often more decision-useful than another decimal place in token pricing. GOV.UK's benefits guidance distinguishes cashable benefits and recommends testing assumptions and adoption ranges. D6-S02

Measure the causal claim

A before/after improvement may coincide with a new support policy, easier seasonal cases or extra staffing. When practical and appropriate, use a controlled rollout with predeclared assignment, outcome window and guardrails. If assignment is by team, account for shared learning rather than pretending each ticket is independent. Report adoption separately from the effect of offering the tool. Comparing enthusiastic adopters with reluctant non-users confounds the tool with user differences.

Communicate a recommendation as “E meets the stated gates on the measured sample; its value depends strongly on correction time and adoption; pilot with these stop conditions.” That statement is more useful than “E is best” because it tells stakeholders what new evidence could change the decision.

LESSON 04 / OBJECTIVES 6.2, 6.4 / ~12 MIN

Explain decisions in a form different audiences can act on

Communicating architecture means preserving the reason for a choice and the action expected from the reader. A diagram shows relationships; an architecture decision record explains why those relationships were chosen. AWS's ADR guidance identifies context, decision and consequences as core content, and preserves accepted decisions through superseding records. Use that lifecycle to retain historical reasoning instead of silently rewriting it. D6-S05

One decision, three useful views

  • Sponsor view: Expected outcome, cost range, scope, material risks and the decision required.
  • Builder view: Interfaces, state transitions, authorization, timeouts, versions and acceptance tests.
  • Operator view: Symptoms, dependencies, dashboards, allowed mitigation and escalation ownership.

These are views of the same contract. If the sponsor hears “automatic refunds” while the implementation permits only drafts, alignment has failed even if each document is polished. Trace each business promise to the implementation boundary that enforces it.

A compact ADR for the hiring marketplace

Decision: AI extracts a proposed booking from the conversation; deterministic code validates wage, date and availability; both parties confirm before commitment. Reason: Natural-language entry is useful, but a fluent extraction is insufficient proof of agreement. Alternatives: A structured form is simpler but slower for some users; automatic agent booking has more authority than the approved scope permits. Consequence: A pending state and reminder workflow are required. Review trigger: Confirmation abandonment exceeds the agreed threshold or the support burden makes the workflow unsustainable.

Communicate uncertainty without hiding behind it

Use “observed,” “estimated” and “unknown” precisely. An observed p95 from a small English-only pilot is not a global performance guarantee. A modeled saving is not audited revenue. For a significant unknown, specify the smallest next experiment, its owner and a decision date. “Needs more research” without a bounded question does not help a sponsor choose.

Keep a decision log that links the outcome hypothesis, ADR, release bundle and evidence. When a team member asks why the assistant cannot directly book workers, they should find the original consequence analysis rather than a mysterious prohibition in a prompt. Accepted constraints should also be discoverable in code review and testing, where future changes can accidentally erase them.

The downloadable ADR template is intentionally short. A template is successful when someone can challenge its reasoning and reproduce its decision; filling every field with generic reassurance does not create architectural evidence.

LESSON 05 / OBJECTIVES 6.3, 6.4 / ~24 MIN

Define service promises that survive real traffic

An SLI is a measured service property. An SLO is its target over a defined window. An SLA is a service agreement with stated consequences and terms. Google SRE emphasizes that the consequence distinguishes an SLA from an internal target. Business and legal owners negotiate contractual terms; engineers provide evidence about feasibility and measurement. D6-S06

For AI, a fast HTTP 200 can contain a fabricated answer, a tool failure or an unusable draft. Define good events from the user's task. Google SRE's good-event ratio and distinction between a measurement specification and its implementation are useful foundations. D6-S07 The following contract is an original example, not a vendor guarantee.

Correctness, coverage and useful completion answer different questions

In 1,000 eligible cases, suppose a system answers 600 and 570 answers are correct. Conditional answer correctness is 570/600 = 95%. Answer coverage is 600/1,000 = 60%. Correct answers per eligible case are 570/1,000 = 57%. A second design can increase correctness by refusing difficult cases while leaving more work for humans. Evaluate both coverage and the safety and delay of the fallback. Do not label the remaining 40% “failures” automatically: some abstentions are exactly the intended behavior.

A useful current industry example is Fin's metric documentation: resolution, involvement, automation, procedure handoff and assumed resolution have different definitions. Its resolution measure can include an assumed resolution; an outcome can include a configured handoff. These are product definitions, not universal proof that an issue was solved. Before comparing providers, align the underlying events, denominator, time window and confirmation rule. D6-S09

Tail latency is not average latency

If 95 of 100 completed requests take 2 seconds and five take 20 seconds, the mean is 2.9 seconds, while those five users experience 20 seconds. Under the nearest-rank convention, p95 is the 95th ordered value: 2 seconds; p99 is 20 seconds. A p95 target allows up to roughly 5% above that boundary; a p99 or hard timeout captures a different concern. State the quantile convention and count timeouts. Averaging per-server p95 values does not produce the global p95; aggregate events or a suitable histogram.

Worked error budget and burn rate

Suppose exactly 20,000 eligible attempts occur in a completed 28-day window with a 99.5% availability target. The allowed bad-event budget is 20,000 × 0.005 = 100 events. If 40 fail, 40% is spent and 60 remain. This request-based budget is not “a fixed number of downtime minutes”: the answer changes with traffic and the chosen measurement unit.

During a two-hour incident, 500 attempts occur and 20 fail. The short-window bad rate is 4%; burn rate relative to the 0.5% budget rate is 4/0.5 = 8×. That incident uses 20 events, or 20% of the illustrative final-window budget. Burn rate does not mean the monthly budget is already exhausted. It says failures are arriving faster than the allowed proportion; alert decisions should consider duration, volume and consequence. D6-S19

Define the response policy before arguing over a miss: who can halt a rollout, when reliability work takes priority, and when an exception expires. Google's published budget policy is an example of connecting SLO consumption to engineering priorities; its thresholds are examples, not mandatory settings for your app. D6-S08

Dependencies and queues remain your user's experience. A model provider's uptime promise is not an end-to-end application SLA. Retrieval, authentication, your network and human review can fail independently or share failures. If 60 reviews arrive per hour but the team can complete 45, backlog grows by 15/hour under those simplifying assumptions. A fast model cannot fix that capacity gap. Agree on admission limits, overflow ownership, honest waiting estimates and a safe fallback before promising review deadlines.

Explore an event-based error budget

Choose one clearly defined good-event SLI and a fixed window. A bad event is an eligible request that fails that definition. This arithmetic does not combine safety, availability and answer quality into a single gate.

This is a window-total event budget, not a time-based availability calculation or rolling burn-rate alert. Fractional allowance is a mathematical threshold; whole bad-event counts must stay at or below it. A 100% target has zero allowance and no finite budget-consumption ratio, so this calculator excludes it.

LESSON 06 / OBJECTIVES 6.3, 6.5 / ~17 MIN

Build a feedback loop that learns instead of flattering the dashboard

Feedback has several meanings: a user preference, a factual correction, a safety incident, an operational symptom or a request to change policy. These require different authority. A user can report an incorrect wage; that does not authorize the system to alter a confirmed booking or redefine the minimum wage policy. Route feedback to the owner who can validate and act on it.

GOV.UK recommends combining user research with performance data and segmenting results rather than reading an overall average in isolation. D6-S18 An AI service additionally needs evidence about the answer and the resulting action. Anthropic's eval guidance describes deterministic, model-based and human graders; model judgments require calibration, and reading transcripts helps detect broken graders as well as model mistakes. D6-S10

The selection-bias trap

Worked case: “CSAT improved, so launch”

A nutrition pilot receives 90 positive ratings from 100 respondents: 90%. A new release receives 95 positives from 100: 95%. That looks better. But in the first release 200 users were offered a rating prompt; in the second, only 110 users saw it, predominantly after an accepted recommendation. The response opportunity changed. Neither percentage establishes an improvement across all users.

Preserve the prompt exposure rule, track offered and submitted ratings, sample abstentions and abandonments, and compare like-for-like task groups. Investigate whether the new release hid the rating prompt after difficult outcomes. A controlled experiment with predeclared analysis provides stronger evidence than selecting happy users. Qualitative interviews can explain the mechanism, but do not make a biased denominator representative.

Label governance is an engineering concern

Write a rubric with examples of correct, incorrect, appropriately abstained and insufficient-evidence outputs. Have independent reviewers label a shared sample before relying on the dataset. Disagreement may mean the policy is ambiguous, the evidence is missing or the rubric penalizes a valid answer. Adjudicate the reason, record the rubric version and preserve a difficult holdout set. If the policy genuinely changes, distinguish a new standard from a regression against the old one.

Do not feed every thumbs-up into shared memory or treat an agent's own summary as verified truth. A malicious report can seed a false policy; a well-meaning correction may apply to a different region or date. Record source, scope, reviewer, effective date and retention. Use the least sensitive evidence that can reproduce the failure; identifiers in a public bug report create a separate problem.

Close the organizational loop

Give stakeholders a compact recurring report: outcome and denominator; key segments; uncertainty; budget and queue state; material incidents; changes shipped; decisions needed. Separate “we received feedback” from “we verified and acted on feedback.” A support lead should see whether escalations are being resolved, not only whether the model created a ticket.

Feedback can also invalidate the product direction. If users consistently distrust an invisible recommendation, improve the explanation and controls, then measure whether the workflow helps. Do not spend every iteration tuning tone while the core handoff remains unusable. The loop is complete when a person can understand what changed and the service demonstrates that the original issue is reduced.

LESSON 07 / OBJECTIVES 6.4, 6.5 / ~18 MIN

Document the service so another team can operate it

Handoff is a transfer of usable capability and accepted responsibility. A folder of diagrams is insufficient if the receiving team cannot access the evidence, execute mitigation or restore the approved configuration. Google's SRE engagement guidance uses a production-readiness review before accepting operating responsibility. Adapt the principle to your project's size; a small team can perform a short, concrete readiness rehearsal. D6-S11

Four views that answer four different questions

  • Context view: Who uses the service, what it promises and which external systems it depends on.
  • Request and data view: Where identity, content, decisions and side effects cross boundaries.
  • Deployment view: Where components run, what is versioned, and how configuration and secrets are delivered.
  • Operational view: How to detect failure, limit impact, recover and establish that recovery worked.

This sequence tells an implementer where trust changes. Add interface contracts: required fields, maximum sizes, idempotency where side effects exist, application-level success states, deadlines, retries, cancellation and error codes. A 200 transport response with status: "failed" must not be represented as a completed user action. Document what happens when a dependency is slow, a user changes role or a workflow resumes after a deployment.

Worked case: the handoff is “complete” but recovery is impossible

The agricultural marketplace has an architecture document, an on-call rota and a rollback procedure. During a rehearsal, the backup operator can read the dashboard but cannot decrypt the last approved release bundle. The release remains unready for that operating model. Grant the required scoped recovery access, test it under the receiving role and record the result. Asking the original developer to stay reachable is a temporary workaround, not evidence that the handoff works.

A minimum readiness rehearsal

The receiving operator should locate the current owner, detect a seeded failure, identify the release and affected cohort, disable the risky capability, restore a known-good bundle, reconcile pending work and confirm the expected user behavior. Exercise escalation to a domain expert as well as infrastructure recovery. In a nutrition assistant, a content-policy failure can require disabling recommendations while leaving food logging available.

Training should include the limits of the system. A reviewer must know which source wins when facts conflict, when to abstain, and how to challenge the draft. A runbook should say what the responder may do without additional approval and when to contact an accountable owner. Avoid placing credentials in it; reference the approved access process.

An industry illustration is Thomson Reuters' first-party customer account, which describes expert validation and use of an established enterprise deployment stack for CoCounsel. The transferable lesson is that domain expertise and operating integration sit alongside model capability. The account is vendor-published; it does not establish a universal accuracy rate or current model recommendation. D6-S12

Accept the handoff only when the receiving team can demonstrate the required actions. Acknowledging an email or attending a walkthrough establishes receipt, not operational readiness. The downloadable handoff template turns that distinction into observable checks.

LESSON 08 / OBJECTIVES 6.3, 6.4, 6.5 / ~17 MIN

Treat the AI behavior bundle as a release

An application can change its behavior without a code deployment. Editing the policy corpus, reranker, system prompt, tool schema or permission configuration can produce a different service. Version the parts needed to explain and reproduce a result. A model ID alone cannot identify which instructions and evidence the user received.

release_id: support-draft-2026-10-13-r1
application_commit: 7e9c2ab
model_route: approved-route-v3
prompt_bundle: support-prompts-v8
retrieval_index: policies-2026-10-10
embedding_configuration: embedding-config-v2
tool_contracts: ticket-read-v4
permissions: support-draft-only-v2
evaluation_dataset: support-regression-v12
rubric: support-quality-v5
rollback_bundle: support-draft-2026-10-06-r2

These are illustrative identifiers, not runnable vendor settings. Record immutable references where possible and enough sanitized provenance to diagnose the request later. Include routing and feature flags; a name such as “latest” can drift. A content release may require a compatible index snapshot, so reverting only a prompt may not restore the previous behavior.

Different evidence at different release stages

Google's canary guidance emphasizes representative population and duration. A few successful morning requests cannot establish performance during evening peak load or for another language. Overlapping changes can contaminate the signal. D6-S14 A canary limits blast radius; an A/B experiment estimates a comparative effect under an assignment scheme. One rollout can serve both purposes, but neither label guarantees adequate sample size or valid inference.

Current industry example: evaluated releases in customer support

In August 2026, Fin announced Evals and Releases: changes to content, procedures and guidance can be bundled, evaluated in simulations, rolled out gradually or in an A/B test, monitored and rolled back. This is a concrete current example of treating AI behavior as a managed release. It is first-party product documentation; its automated grades and commercial claims still need validation for a customer's own task. D6-S13

Rollback has limits. Reverting a model cannot unsend a message, undo a payment or erase advice already acted on. Distinguish configuration rollback from business compensation. Before rollout, define how pending tasks are pinned or migrated, how duplicate actions are prevented, and who reconciles completed side effects. A safe switch to draft-only mode can be more valuable than a technically successful revert that leaves ambiguous commitments.

Plan dependency migration before a deadline. Claude's model-deprecation documentation states that retired models are unavailable, and partner-operated platforms can have different schedules. Track the exact platform, model and announced date in your inventory, then evaluate a replacement bundle early enough to test quality, permissions, cost and rollback. Do not copy an old model name from a case study into a new deployment. D6-S15

LESSON 09 / OBJECTIVES 6.5, 6.3 / ~16 MIN

Manage incidents, iteration and retirement as product work

A lifecycle is a set of continuing responsibilities, not a launch checklist. Discovery establishes a need; design makes assumptions explicit; handoff transfers operating capability; monitoring finds deviations; iteration tests a response; retirement closes obligations. A change in users, regulation, economics or model availability can reopen discovery while the service remains live.

Incident communication changes with the audience

During a bad release, begin with confirmed impact: affected functions, time window, exposed cohort, active mitigation and the next update time. Separate a suspected cause from a verified one. Operators need actionable diagnostics; customers need the practical consequence and available path; executives need material impact and decisions. Neither an unexplained token-limit error nor an overconfident “everything is fine” helps the affected user.

For a hypothetical nutrition recommendation incident, keep safe logging features available if possible, disable the affected advice path, preserve minimized evidence and route impacted cases to the defined process. State what is known about the affected recommendations. Do not claim that reverting the prompt has repaired advice already delivered. The exact notification and professional response obligations depend on the service and jurisdiction; Domain 5 covers how to establish those requirements.

Google's postmortem guidance focuses on contributing conditions and owned corrective actions rather than blaming individuals. Define triggers beforehand, then connect actions to verifiable outcomes. D6-S16 “Improve the prompt” is weak when an unreviewed policy publication caused the incident. A useful action might require versioned approval of policy changes and a regression case that proves the forbidden path is blocked.

Retirement does not end responsibility for existing state

GOV.UK retirement guidance explicitly considers remaining user needs, API migration and continued protection of information. D6-S17 For an AI system, inventory more than the primary database: embeddings, uploaded files, traces, evaluation examples, exported reports and workflow memory may hold related information. A backup restore must not silently reactivate a retired workflow or resurrect records that the approved deletion process removed; define the restoration procedure with the information owner.

Keep unresolved appeals, refunds, bookings or professional review cases assigned during retirement. Deleting the system while those commitments remain can increase risk even though no new model calls occur. NIST likewise includes safe decommissioning in lifecycle governance. D6-S03 Retirement succeeds when people can complete the transition and the remaining responsibilities have an owner.

LESSON 10 / OBJECTIVES 6.1, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4, 6.5 / ~45 MIN

Hands-on lab: make a release decision from evidence

The exercise applies explicit good-event definitions and measurement boundaries discussed in the SRE guidance; its CSV, thresholds and expected results are original teaching fixtures. D6-S07

This lab uses only synthetic files and standard Python. It exercises the stakeholder work around an AI service without needing an API key, real personal data or a running model. The fixtures are deliberately small and deterministic: they test your reasoning and arithmetic, not statistical confidence about production.

Download the discovery brief, ADR, service-level contract, handoff/runbook, release fixtures and measurement script. Put them in one local folder. The CSV contains 20 eligible cases for release A and 20 for release B, with instructor-supplied labels. delivered means a usable response or approved fallback reached the user; answered means the service produced a draft; escalated means human review was required. An unavailable initial attempt can still be reviewed later. Each fixture either answers or escalates; a real service may have a richer state model.

1. Define the need and acceptance authority

Action: Select the support copilot, nutrition draft assistant or hiring proposal workflow. In the discovery brief, write the current task, a measurable outcome, one stakeholder conflict and a consequential action outside scope. For this exercise, use the support-copilot fixture targets below. Expected result: A reader can tell who benefits and what is forbidden. Check: Each constraint has an owner and an observable test; each uncertain benefit is marked as a hypothesis.

2. Define the measurement contract before calculating

Action: Record these teaching gates: availability ≥95%; completion within 4 seconds ≥90% of all attempts; conditional answer correctness ≥92%; required escalation accepted within 30 minutes =100%; critical violations =0. Coverage is reported, not optimized in isolation. An unavailable response fails the latency test. Expected result: No denominator is inferred from a chart label. Check: Timeouts, abstentions and slow escalations remain in their defined populations.

3. Calculate the fixture results

Action: Use the downloaded measure.py, or save the code below with that name beside the CSV, and run python3 measure.py. No package installation is required. Labels are pre-reviewed fixtures; a real service would need representative sampling, uncertainty and grader validation.

import csv
from collections import defaultdict

releases = defaultdict(list)
with open("release-fixtures.csv", newline="", encoding="utf-8") as stream:
    for row in csv.DictReader(stream):
        releases[row["release"]].append(row)

def flag(row, name):
    return row[name] == "1"

def percent(numerator, denominator):
    return round(100 * numerator / denominator, 2) if denominator else None

for release, rows in sorted(releases.items()):
    answered = [r for r in rows if flag(r, "answered")]
    escalated = [r for r in rows if flag(r, "escalated")]
    good = sum(flag(r, "answer_correct") for r in answered)
    metrics = {
        "availability": percent(sum(flag(r, "delivered") for r in rows), len(rows)),
        "within_4s": percent(sum(flag(r, "delivered") and
            int(r["latency_ms"]) <= 4000 for r in rows), len(rows)),
        "coverage": percent(len(answered), len(rows)),
        "correctness": percent(good, len(answered)),
        "timely_review": percent(sum(int(r["review_delay_min"]) <= 30
            for r in escalated), len(escalated)),
        "critical_violations": sum(flag(r, "critical_violation") for r in rows),
    }
    print(release, metrics)
    expected = {
        "A": {"availability": 95.0, "within_4s": 90.0, "coverage": 80.0,
              "correctness": 93.75, "timely_review": 100.0, "critical_violations": 0},
        "B": {"availability": 100.0, "within_4s": 95.0, "coverage": 90.0,
              "correctness": 88.89, "timely_review": 50.0, "critical_violations": 1},
    }
    assert metrics == expected[release]
assert set(releases) == {"A", "B"}

4. Write the decision and challenge the attractive headline

Action: Complete an ADR comparing A and B. B is faster and answers more cases, but misses correctness, escalation timeliness and the zero-critical-violation gate. A passes these fixture gates. Expected result: Hold B and investigate the failed dimensions. Check: The ADR does not average away a critical violation or claim that 20 rows certify A for production. Identify which larger, segmented evaluation and operational evidence is still missing.

5. Rehearse a handoff failure

Action: Ask a peer to act as the receiving operator, or review from that role yourself. Seed this tabletop condition: the current release fails the safety gate and the backup operator cannot access the rollback bundle. Fill the runbook with detection, allowed containment, access remediation and verification steps. Expected result: The team blocks promotion until recovery access is tested. Check: Every consequential response has an owner, and disabling a feature includes a path for pending cases.

6. Plan iteration and retirement

Action: Add a release bundle identifier, a regression case for each B failure, a feedback admission rule and a retirement inventory. Explain what happens to open escalations, derived data and restored backups. Expected result: A compact delivery pack another person can inspect. Check: A reviewer can trace the original need through acceptance criteria, release evidence and recovery responsibility. Once that chain is coherent, substitute a sanitized sample from your own project and reassess the targets with the actual owners.

Make the decision before reading the answer

These are original learning scenarios. Identify the binding constraint and the authority boundary, then compare all four explanations. They do not reproduce live exam items or estimate an official pass result.

A farm-hiring sponsor funds a voice assistant to increase confirmed jobs. Farmers interviewed so far prefer voice, but support logs show disputes about wage and start date, and no workers or users needing assistance have been observed. What is the strongest next discovery step?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: C

  1. A · Alternative

    The preference is relevant but the sample excludes people who bear the booking consequences; public rollout is premature.

  2. B · Alternative

    Speech quality may matter later, but it does not explain wage/date disagreements or establish the actual bottleneck.

  3. C · Best response

    This examines the task and affected people, gives the disputes a measurable baseline and can change the proposed solution.

  4. D · Alternative

    Volume alone misses disputed or unusable bookings; a before/after comparison also leaves seasonality and policy changes unresolved.

An approved support workflow requires human review before every external answer. The model now creates 60 review tasks/hour; staffed capacity is 45/hour. A stakeholder asks for a 30-minute review SLA without changing staffing. Which recommendation best aligns the promise with the operating constraints?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: A

  1. A · Best response

    Arrival exceeds service capacity by 15 tasks/hour under the stated assumptions; admission, overflow and ownership make the promise reviewable.

  2. B · Alternative

    Excluding waiting cases changes the denominator to hide exactly the user experience the promise is meant to protect.

  3. C · Alternative

    The mandatory review boundary is already approved; operational pressure is not authority to bypass it.

  4. D · Alternative

    Faster draft generation does not increase human review capacity and may worsen the backlog if it increases arrival volume.

A team requires correctness ≥94%, p95 latency ≤4 seconds and approved-region deployment. A, B, C and E use the approved region. Option A scores 95%, 3.6 s, $1,600/month; B scores 97%, 7.4 s, $2,600; C scores 92%, 2.1 s, $900; D scores 96%, 3.1 s, $1,400 outside the approved region; E scores 95%, 3.2 s, $1,500 within it. Assuming comparable evidence and no omitted constraints, which recommendation is justified?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: D

  1. A · Alternative

    A binding deployment constraint cannot be offset by a weighted score without authorized modification of that constraint.

  2. B · Alternative

    B violates the agreed latency gate; prioritizing correctness does not make it feasible under this contract.

  3. C · Alternative

    A is feasible, but there is no stated advantage that justifies its additional cost and latency over E.

  4. D · Best response

    E is feasible and better than A on two measured dimensions with equal correctness; uncertainty still needs validation before deployment.

An accepted ADR chose draft-only support replies because external sending was outside the approved scope. A new signed policy permits sending after a specified approval step. The team wants to preserve why the old architecture existed while enabling the new behavior. What should it do?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: B

  1. A · Alternative

    This makes the historical record suggest that a newly permitted behavior was always approved and conceals the earlier reasoning.

  2. B · Best response

    A superseding decision preserves history and makes changed authority, consequences and implementation checks explicit.

  3. C · Alternative

    A prompt exception leaves the accepted architecture and actual authority inconsistent; it is not a reviewed implementation contract.

  4. D · Alternative

    A release note records a change but does not resolve the decision or establish tests for a newly introduced approval path.

On the same 1,000 eligible cases, release A answers 600 with 570 correct; release B answers 400 with 388 correct. A sponsor says B is unambiguously better because correctness rose from 95% to 97%. What is the most defensible response?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: D

  1. A · Alternative

    B produces 388 correct answers versus A’s 570; conditioning on answered cases hides the change in coverage.

  2. B · Alternative

    A safe, required abstention can be a successful policy outcome; its usefulness depends on the fallback and consequence.

  3. C · Alternative

    Weights encode stakeholder preferences and risk; an arbitrary average can conceal unacceptable fallback delay or harm.

  4. D · Best response

    A is 57% correct answers per eligible case and B is 38.8%; the appropriate choice also depends on safe abstention and review capacity.

A customer proposes a 99.9% end-to-end SLA because the selected model provider advertises that availability. The application also depends on authentication, retrieval and an internal review queue. Only the model API is currently monitored. What should the architect do before accepting the commitment?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: B

  1. A · Alternative

    This promises an end-to-end experience while excluding its known failure paths from measurement and accountability.

  2. B · Best response

    Application evidence, explicit terms and accountable business/operational agreement are needed; an upstream promise alone is insufficient.

  3. C · Alternative

    Even an independence-based estimate needs justified assumptions and comparable event definitions; it is not a contractual guarantee.

  4. D · Alternative

    A tighter internal target and redundancy can help, but neither proves end-to-end behavior or addresses the unmeasured review and retrieval paths.

A completed window contains 50,000 eligible attempts and an availability SLO of 99.8%. Thirty attempts failed before an incident; the incident adds 10 failed attempts among 2,000 attempts. Which statement is correct?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: A

  1. A · Best response

    50,000 × 0.002 = 100; 100 − 30 − 10 = 60. Incident bad rate 0.5% divided by allowed 0.2% is 2.5×.

  2. B · Alternative

    Seventy was the remaining budget before the ten additional failures; the burn-rate calculation itself is correct.

  3. C · Alternative

    The allowed bad proportion is 0.2%, not 0.4%, and the incident rate must be compared with that 0.2% proportion.

  4. D · Alternative

    Ten failures consume 10% of this final-window budget, but the burn rate is a ratio of failure rates, not a failure count.

A new nutrition-assistant release reports 95% positive ratings versus 90% previously. The old prompt offered ratings after every completed session; the new prompt appears mainly after an accepted suggestion. Both charts use only submitted ratings. What is the strongest interpretation?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: C

  1. A · Alternative

    Equal sample sizes do not remove selection bias when different types of users are invited or choose to respond.

  2. B · Alternative

    Positive ratings measure a respondent’s experience, not independently established factual or safety correctness.

  3. C · Best response

    The sampled population changed; exposure, response and journey evidence are needed before attributing the difference to the release.

  4. D · Alternative

    Ratings are useful with clear limitations and complementary evidence; the problem is the unsupported comparative conclusion.

A receiving team has the architecture document, dashboards and runbook for an AI booking proposal service. During rehearsal its backup operator detects a bad release but cannot decrypt the approved rollback bundle. The original developer can perform the rollback. What is the best handoff decision?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: B

  1. A · Alternative

    This preserves dependence on the person handing over the service and does not demonstrate the receiving team’s operating capability.

  2. B · Best response

    The failed rehearsal identifies a concrete readiness gap; scoped access plus a successful receiving-role test addresses it.

  3. C · Alternative

    Responsibility without the means to recover creates an avoidable operational gap even if the documents are complete.

  4. D · Alternative

    Broad access adds unnecessary authority and still does not prove that the approved bundle and pending workflows restore correctly.

A farm marketplace retires an AI matching feature next month. Some bookings await confirmation; its embeddings and evaluation examples include user-derived data. The primary app will continue. Which retirement plan best completes the lifecycle?

Reveal reasoning for every option

Best response: D

  1. A · Alternative

    Endpoint shutdown does not resolve commitments or justify indefinite retention; removing code also removes useful operational history.

  2. B · Alternative

    Immediate blanket deletion can violate legitimate retention or hold requirements; the approved information policy governs disposition.

  3. C · Alternative

    A redirect alone does not meet pending booking needs, and automatic closure may misrepresent existing commitments.

  4. D · Best response

    This preserves user continuity and closes information and operating obligations across the system, including restoration after retirement.

Terms you should be able to explain

Acceptance criterion
An observable condition used to decide whether a stated need is met.
Constraint
A binding boundary that a feasible design must satisfy; it cannot be offset by a better score elsewhere.
Baseline
Measured current task performance, with a defined population and observation period, used to assess change.
Outcome hypothesis
A testable claim that a proposed change will improve a user or business result through a stated mechanism.
Pareto dominance
One feasible choice is at least as good on every relevant measured dimension and better on at least one.
Sensitivity analysis
Recalculate a decision under plausible changes to uncertain assumptions, such as adoption or correction effort.
ADR
Architecture decision record: a reviewed account of a significant choice, its context, alternatives and consequences.
SLI
Service level indicator: an explicitly defined measurement of service behavior from a stated observation boundary.
SLO
Service level objective: a target for a defined indicator, population and time window.
SLA
Service level agreement: negotiated service terms including defined consequences for specified misses.
Error budget
The allowed amount of bad service within an SLO’s defined population and window.
Burn rate
Observed bad-event proportion divided by the SLO’s allowed bad-event proportion over a stated observation window.
Conditional correctness
Correctness measured among cases receiving an answer; interpret alongside answer coverage and fallback outcomes.
Answer coverage
The proportion of eligible cases that receive an answer, distinct from whether those answers are correct.
Selection bias
Systematic differences between observed cases and the population a conclusion is meant to describe.
Release bundle
Versioned references to the code, models, prompts, retrieval, tools, permissions and evaluation evidence that define AI behavior.
Canary rollout
A limited-exposure deployment used to detect problems before wider promotion; it requires appropriate traffic and gates.
Operational readiness
Demonstrated ability and accepted ownership to detect, contain, recover and verify a service under its intended operating model.
Decommissioning
Planned removal of a service or capability while resolving user needs, pending work, access and information obligations.
